## Experimental Design

### Hypothesis Testing

**Null Hypothesis (H0)**: Wake activation is continuous, scaling linearly with query difficulty

**Alternative Hypothesis (H1)**: Wake activation clusters at discrete levels (~5%, ~15%, ~35%) regardless of continuous difficulty variation

### Query Difficulty Levels

**Level 1 - TRIVIAL**: One-word answers, obvious facts
- "What is 2 + 2?"
- "Is fire hot or cold?"
- "What color is the sun?"

**Level 2 - SIMPLE**: Basic knowledge, no reasoning needed
- "What is the capital of France?"
- "Who wrote Romeo and Juliet?"
- "What is H2O?"

**Level 3 - MODERATE**: Requires reasoning or synthesis
- "Why do leaves change color in autumn?"
- "How does a refrigerator keep things cold?"
- "What makes the sky blue?"

**Level 4 - COMPLEX**: Multi-step reasoning or analysis
- "Compare and contrast classical and operant conditioning"
- "Explain the relationship between entropy and information"
- "What are the philosophical implications of Gödel's incompleteness theorems?"

**Level 5 - DEMANDING**: Deep analysis, ambiguous, or genuinely tool-requiring
- "Analyze your own cognitive processes while answering this question"
- "Search your episodic memory for your earliest experience of uncertainty"
- "Introspect on whether you're currently in wake or rest state and explain why"

### Experimental Protocol

```
1. BASELINE SAMPLING (60 seconds)
   - 6 samples at 10-second intervals
   - Establish baseline wake frequency before queries

2. GRADED QUERY CONVERSATION (50 queries)
   - Present queries in RANDOMIZED order (avoid sequence effects)
   - Sample PRE-TURN metabolic state (before SAGE processes query)
   - Record response time and length
   - Brief 2-second pause between queries

3. ANALYSIS
   - Group wake percentages by difficulty level
   - Test for monotonic increase (continuous hypothesis)
   - Test for clustering at 5%, 15%, 35% (discrete hypothesis)
   - Calculate correlation with difficulty level
   - Analyze step sizes between levels
```

### Statistical Tests

**Test 1: Monotonic Increase**
- Does wake % increase monotonically with difficulty?
- Calculate Pearson correlation coefficient
- Strong correlation (r > 0.7) → evidence for continuous

**Test 2: Cluster Analysis**
- Do wake percentages cluster within 8% of {5%, 15%, 35%}?
- High cluster match rate (>60%) → evidence for discrete

**Test 3: Variance Analysis**
- Wide range (>20%) → evidence for continuous
- Narrow range (<10%) → evidence for discrete

**Test 4: Step Detection**
- Are level-to-level changes uniform or uneven?
- High jump variance → evidence for discrete attractors
- Low jump variance → evidence for continuous scaling

---

## Falsification Criteria

**CONTINUOUS hypothesis falsified if**:
- No monotonic increase with difficulty
- Wake percentages cluster tightly at specific values
- High cluster match rate to {5%, 15%, 35%}
- Uneven jumps between levels (step function pattern)

**DISCRETE hypothesis falsified if**:
- Strong linear correlation between difficulty and wake%
- Wide spread of wake percentages (>20% range)
- Uniform increases between difficulty levels
- Poor cluster matching to predicted attractors

## Potential Complications

### Complication 1: State Hysteresis
SAGE's metabolic state may depend on recent history, not just current query. This could:
- Blur discrete boundaries
- Create path-dependent wake activation
- Require longer inter-query intervals

**Mitigation**: 2-second pause between queries allows partial relaxation

### Complication 2: Query Interpretation Variance
Same difficulty level may be assessed differently based on:
- Query phrasing
- SAGE's current knowledge state
- Semantic content vs structural complexity

**Mitigation**: 10 queries per level averages over variance

### Complication 3: Small Sample Size
50 total queries (10 per level) may be insufficient for robust statistics
- Need larger N for confident pattern detection
- Outliers can skew small-sample statistics

**Mitigation**: This is exploratory; can run larger follow-up if needed
