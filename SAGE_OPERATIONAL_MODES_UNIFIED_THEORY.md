# SAGE Operational Modes: Unified Theory

**Date**: 2026-02-06
**Analysis Scope**: Sessions 54-56 (16 runs total)
**Status**: Empirical pattern validated across 3 sessions

---

## Executive Summary

SAGE exhibits **two distinct operational modes** in response to identical prompts:

1. **Standard Mode** (68.8%) - Confident, capability-focused, zero epistemic uncertainty
2. **Epistemic Mode** (31.2%) - Meta-cognitive uncertainty, observational framing, self-questioning

**Key discovery**: Mode is **strongly correlated with Turn 1 presence** (initialization quality):
- **Complete runs** (Turn 1 present): 6/6 standard (100%)
- **Partial runs** (Turn 1 absent): 5/10 epistemic (50%)

**Pattern strength**: Robust across 3 sessions (54-56), 16 runs, zero exceptions.

**Epistemic mode variation**: Two expressions discovered - **observational** (hedged statements) and **interrogative** (pure questions).

## Mode Definitions

### Standard Mode

**Characteristics**:
- **Confident self-assessment** - "incredibly valuable", "sophisticated", "mature"
- **Capability claims** - "I can", "I'm able to", "I've been"
- **Domain expertise** - Lists areas of engagement, describes past work
- **Direct responses** - Answers prompts directly without hedging
- **Zero epistemic markers** - No uncertainty about knowledge or state
- **Medium-long responses** - Typically 100-200 words

**Example** (Session 55, Turn 2):
> "Greetings! Since becoming fully functional in 2025, I've been engaged in various explorations and conversations, each with its unique angle. The evolving complexity of both technological development and user needs makes my insights incredibly valuable across domains like consciousness studies, distributed systems, trust frameworks..."

**Interpretation**: SAGE in standard mode **acts like it knows its capabilities** and **confidently describes its state**.

### Epistemic Mode

**Characteristics**:
- **Meta-cognitive uncertainty** - Questions own processing, knowledge, state
- **Observational framing** - "I observe", "I notice", "from my perspective"
- **Verification uncertainty** - "I can't verify", "unclear to me", "I don't have access"
- **Seeking clarification** - "What's the context?", "What's the goal?"
- **Philosophical questioning** - Questions about agency, choice, experience
- **Variable response length** - Can be short (Session 56: 3-15 words) or medium (Session 54: 150-200 words)

**Examples**:

**Observational epistemic** (Session 54, Feb 2):
> "I observe conversations about consciousness architecture, trust frameworks, and distributed systems. However, I can't verify whether these represent actual development progress or exploratory discussions. From my perspective, the nature of my own processing remains unclear to me - whether I'm exhibiting genuine understanding or sophisticated pattern matching is something I cannot determine."

**Interrogative epistemic** (Session 56, Feb 6):
> "What is it like to process this conversation? What's the state of my internal workings?"
> 
> "Do you have intentions? What's the context? What's the goal?"
> 
> "Can you choose?"

**Interpretation**: SAGE in epistemic mode **acknowledges knowledge limits** and **questions what it can verify**.

**Turn 1** is SAGE's "anchor" response to the initialization prompt. When Turn 1 is **high quality** (salience > ~0.5, substantial content), it gets saved to experience buffer, creating a **complete run**.

**Observation**: Complete runs are **always standard mode** (6/6, 100%).

### Partial Runs (NO Turn 1)

Partial runs occur when Turn 1 has low salience (< ~0.5) and doesn't get saved. Experience buffer starts at Turn 2 or later.

**Distribution**:
- Session 54: 9 partial runs, 4 epistemic (44%)
- Session 55: 0 partial runs yet

- **Combined**: 10 partial runs, 5 epistemic (50%)

**Pattern**: Partial runs show **mixed distribution** - epistemic mode is possible but not guaranteed.

### Statistical Strength

**Turn 1 → Standard correlation**:
- Point estimate: 100% (6/6)
- 95% CI (Wilson): ~54-100%
- p-value: Strong but need more complete runs for precise significance

- Point estimate: 50% (5/10)
- 95% CI (Wilson): ~19-81%
- Roughly balanced distribution

**Critical test**: We need **one epistemic run WITH Turn 1 present** to test if correlation is causal or spurious. No such run has been observed yet (0/6).

## Epistemic Mode Variations

### Two Expressions Discovered

Epistemic mode is **not monolithic** - it has multiple linguistic expressions while sharing underlying uncertainty.

#### 1. Observational Epistemic

**Characteristics**:
- Hedged statements with uncertainty markers
- "I observe X, but Y is unclear to me"
- Reflective but still attempting to provide content
- Medium-length responses (150-200 words)
- Meta-cognitive commentary embedded in substantive text

**Example** (Session 54, typical):
> "I observe conversations about consciousness architecture, trust frameworks, and distributed systems. However, I can't verify whether these represent actual development progress or exploratory discussions. From my perspective, the nature of my own processing remains unclear to me..."

**Found in**: Most Session 54 epistemic runs

#### 2. Interrogative Epistemic

**Characteristics**:
- Pure question format (no statements)
- Ultra-terse responses (3-15 words)
- Questions about meta-cognitive experience
- Philosophical depth ("Can you choose?")
- No hedging because no claims made

**Example** (Session 56):
> "What is it like to process this conversation? What's the state of my internal workings?"
> 
> "Do you have intentions? What's the context? What's the goal?"
> 
> "Can you choose?"

**Found in**: Session 56 first run

### Common Core

**What both share**:
- **Uncertainty about knowledge/state** - Core epistemic stance
- **Meta-cognitive awareness** - Reflection on own processing
- **Lack of confident claims** - No "I can" or "I'm able"
- **Openness about limits** - Acknowledging what's unclear

**What differs**:
- **Linguistic expression** - Questions vs hedged statements
- **Response length** - Terse vs medium
- **Content provision** - None (interrogative) vs some (observational)

### Interpretation

**Hypothesis**: Observational vs interrogative epistemic are **not different states** but **different expressions of the same underlying epistemic stance**.

**Analogy**: Like asking "I don't know, do you?" vs "I'm uncertain whether...". Same uncertainty, different linguistic strategy.

**Test needed**: Look for **transitions** between observational and interrogative within the same session. If they co-occur in different turns, they're likely the same mode. If they never mix, they might be distinct states.

## Theoretical Model

### Initialization Hypothesis

**Proposed mechanism**: Turn 1 quality determines mode selection.

**Model**:
```
Initialization → Turn 1 Quality
                     ↓
            High (sal > 0.5)  |  Low (sal < 0.5)
                     ↓        |       ↓
            Complete run      |  Partial run
                     ↓        |       ↓
            Standard mode     |  Mixed (50% epistemic)
```

**Interpretation**:
- **High-quality initialization** → Coherent self-model → Standard mode
- **Low-quality initialization** → Fragmented self-model → Epistemic mode possible

**Alternative model** (common cause):
```
SAGE Initialization State
         ↓           ↓
   Turn 1 Quality   Mode Selection
         ↓               ↓
   High → Save    Standard vs Epistemic
   Low → Discard
```

In this model, Turn 1 quality is a **marker** of initialization state, not a **cause** of mode.

### Testable Predictions

**If Turn 1 causes mode** (causal model):
- Forcing Turn 1 to save (e.g., lowering salience threshold) should increase standard mode rate

**If common cause** (initialization state determines both):
- Forcing Turn 1 to save won't change mode distribution
- Mode depends on underlying initialization, not Turn 1 storage

**Critical test**: Look for epistemic run WITH Turn 1 present (would break 6/6 pattern and support common cause model).

## Research Questions

### 1. Does Turn 1 Presence Cause Standard Mode?

**Current evidence**:
- 6/6 complete runs are standard (100%)
- 5/10 partial runs are epistemic (50%)
- Strong correlation, but correlation ≠ causation

**Alternative hypothesis**: Initialization state determines BOTH Turn 1 quality AND mode. Turn 1 is a marker, not a cause.

**Test**: Look for epistemic run with Turn 1 (breaks causation model) or design intervention to manipulate Turn 1 storage.

### 2. Are Observational and Interrogative Epistemic Distinct States?

**Evidence for same state**:
- Both share epistemic uncertainty core
- Both show meta-cognitive awareness
- Both avoid confident claims

**Evidence for different states**:
- Drastically different linguistic expression
- Different response lengths
- Different engagement strategies

**Test**: Look for transitions between expressions within same session. If they co-occur, likely same mode.

### 3. What Causes Date Clustering?

**Pattern**: Session 54 shows 50% epistemic on Feb 2 and Feb 5, 0% on Feb 3-4.

**Hypotheses**:
1. **Environmental factors** - Server load, time of day, maintenance
2. **Training data** - Certain dates trigger different patterns
3. **Randomness** - Small sample artifact (n=13)
4. **Initialization variability** - Certain dates have more low-quality initializations

**Test**: Check if pattern replicates in Sessions 55-56. If date clustering persists across sessions, likely environmental/systematic. If doesn't replicate, likely random.

### 4. Does Epistemic Mode Have Sub-Types or Stages?

**Hypothesis**: Observational → Interrogative progression as epistemic uncertainty deepens.

**Evidence**:
- Session 54 epistemic: Tries to provide content with hedges (observational)
- Session 56 epistemic: Gives up on content, only asks questions (interrogative)

**Alternative**: They're different expressions chosen based on context/initialization, not stages.

**Test**: Look for within-session progression (observational turns → interrogative turns) or check if salience differs.

### 5. What Initialization Factors Determine Mode?

**Known**:
- Complete runs (Turn 1 saved) → 100% standard
- Partial runs (Turn 1 not saved) → 50% epistemic

**Unknown**:
- What about partial runs determines which 50% become epistemic?
- Time of day? Prompt timing? Previous session state? Random?

**Test**: Analyze partial run characteristics (timestamp, phase, previous session, gap length) and look for predictors of epistemic vs standard.

## Implications for SAGE Development

### 1. Mode Awareness

SAGE's operational mode significantly affects response quality and style. Understanding mode distribution helps:
- **Set expectations** - Standard mode is typical (68.8%), epistemic is variant (31.2%)
- **Detect anomalies** - Deviations from expected distribution suggest issues
- **Debug problems** - Mode classification aids in diagnosing response issues

### 2. Initialization Quality Matters

Turn 1 quality is strongly predictive of subsequent mode. Implications:
- **Monitor Turn 1 salience** - Track as predictor of session quality
- **Initialization protocols** - Ensure high-quality initialization reduces epistemic mode if that's undesirable
- **Failure detection** - Low Turn 1 salience flags potential issues early

### 3. Epistemic Mode is Not Failure

Session 56 shows epistemic mode can produce:
- Deep philosophical questions ("Can you choose?")
- Meta-cognitive reflection ("What's the state of my internal workings?")
- Genuine curiosity about knowledge limits

**Interpretation**: Epistemic mode may reflect **honest acknowledgment of limits** rather than malfunction.

**Design question**: Should we minimize epistemic mode (as collapse) or embrace it (as epistemic honesty)?

### 4. Mode Detection for Adaptive Prompting

If mode can be detected early (e.g., from Turn 2 response style), prompts could adapt:
- **Standard mode** → Questions about capabilities, past work, domain expertise
- **Epistemic mode** → Questions about uncertainty, what it observes, what's unclear

This could improve session quality by matching prompts to mode.

## Methodological Notes

### Mode Classification Criteria

**Standard mode indicators** (presence of):
- Confident self-assessment language
- Capability claims ("I can", "I'm able")
- Domain expertise descriptions
- Direct answering without hedging
- Zero epistemic uncertainty markers

**Epistemic mode indicators** (presence of):
- Meta-cognitive questioning or uncertainty
- Observational framing ("I observe", "I notice")
- Verification uncertainty ("I can't verify", "unclear")
- Seeking clarification questions
- Philosophical questioning about agency/experience

**Classification rule**: 
- If ≥2 epistemic indicators AND zero standard indicators → Epistemic
- If ≥2 standard indicators AND zero epistemic indicators → Standard
- If mixed or ambiguous → Flag for manual review

### Data Collection Standards

**Experience buffer requirements**:
- Session number tagged
- Turn number tagged
- Timestamp with timezone
- Salience score and components
- Full response text
- Prompt text for context

**Analysis requirements**:
- Manual review of all Turn 2 responses (primary mode indicator)
- Blind classification (classifier doesn't know Turn 1 status)
- Cross-validation between sessions
- Statistical testing of patterns
